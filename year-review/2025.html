2025 in review



Here we go again. I'm writing this introduction in March 14th 2026 and the Oscars are tomorrow.I couldn't care less for the Oscars other than to be happy for wins from whom I think deserves it, if they are even nominated (this year Wagner Moura and O Agente Secreto would be my great celebrations), but it is in many ways a milestone. I feel a strange obligation to finish this year review, that I have been tinkering with for months, before them, otherwise I may never do it, and I'd certainly regret that. Third time doing this, and already last year I was worried about being able to keep this up, so of course that I thought reasonable to (1) continue to not start early and (2) decide to do even more things than I was doing before (like a whole top 100 for the last 25y). That the last two months were some of the busiest I've ever had with work did not help, also. Well, one way or another let's get this done.

In the last months I found myself thinking a lot about two quotes from great films I saw last year:

From here on in it's one battle after another.

Feels like 2025 (but really, applies for any year). And:

La vida es como la espuma, por eso hay que darse como el mar.

Feels like life. The image of Maribel Verdú going into the sea came unbidden to my mind costantly.

2025 had me worried at times about whether I'd be able to keep up my movie watching the way I like it, i.e., going a lot to movie theaters and watching lots of films, both old and new. The former is crucial because seating in the dark in a movie theater, locked in, is one of my favorite things. The latter is what allows me to be able to have a representative view of newer films, to reduce the number of holes in my old-movie awareness, and to rewatch films, which is good both for films that I like, allowing a deeper experience, and to reevaluate films that did not hit quite right the first time.

It all ended well though. I saw lots of films (182 in total) and went 76 times to a movie theather, my most ever. The number of great films from 2025 was not that impressive, though, leading to a weaker "Best of" list (always under construction) than that of 2024, but that did not change the fact that I saw a lot of great cinema, ever renewing my passion for this absolute art form.More than once in 2025 I complained to other CS researchers about my worry that GenAI will hurt cinema. Because funders want to cut corners and mediocre art automatically generated will be pushed harder. Canalhas. We must not settle for this. But the encroachment persists. Note that this is not something about the technology improving or whatever. It is about thinking that it'll be interesting to remove humans from the process. It's the idiosyncratic human experience that makes cinema what it is. And as Ted Chiang says, experience is algorithmically incompressible. By the way, ironically, how to more easily add such footnotes to this review, and I do use a lot of footnotes, was just taught to me by Claude, magically, from a single prompt. Blessed by thy name. But I'm not looking forward to watch your movies, Claude.

The Journey

I was on vacation until mid-January and away from Belo Horizonte, and ended up watching only one film in the period, while visiting my family in Sousa,Terra dos dinossauros! But insanely hot in early January... I was really worried about how people are gonna still be living there 30 years from now, when instead of around 40°C it'll be like around 50°C that time of the year. so it was not until the Week 3 that movie-watching got going in earnest. Historically, i.e., discousting the outiliers 2015, 2016, so from 2017 to 2025, January is my strongest month, averaging just above 20 movies. But in the last years the more common thing is for it to be around the overall average of ~15, with a mix of vacation, work travel, and paper deadlines eroding its potential. From Week 3 to Week 10, i.e., mid January to early March, I saw 39 films, for an average of 4.88 films per week, and 18 films seen in a cinema. Week 8, with a single watched film, is an outlier in this period and I was wondering why, but seemed to remember that it was due to a crazy week with a paper deadline. Checking my time logs from that week I confirmed that. Deadline weeks are the worst. I was cathing up with a lot of 2024 films I had not seen yet, taking another look at some I had already seen, catching up to some incredible 2023 ones, and enjoying Cine Humberto Mauro's series on summer movies. This pace is way above my usual one, and it could not last.

From mid-March to mid-April I had a couple 1-film weeks and a few "standard" weeks This is not quite true since in an average week I generally watch closer to 4 films and go closer to 1.5 times to a cinema. But it's how I think of them. of 3 watched films, being 1 in a cinema. Then the next four weeks (Week 16 to 19) had again a high average, 5 per week, and lots of cinema, 12 times, including Sinners, the first amazing film from 2025, and Warfare, a film that really stayed with me. This pace was again greatly helped by a Cine Humerto Mauro series, this one on Leone's films. These months in general I was also helped by a regular routine, without travel and with work going kinda normal. The weekly average by this point sat at a very good 3.73. That was about to change though.

May came with increasing work obligations and then in June and July I traveled a lot, for work and for vacation. Historically June is my weakest month, with an average just below 10, and July is close by, together with September and October, with averages between 10 and 11. The Northern Hemisphere Summer concentrates the scientific events in my field and I'm often traveling for work. June is also hurt by the end of the teaching semester, which is quite busy and moreover I often take vacation after that. This year was no different. In June, and the first week of July, I still managed to go the cinema a few times, Incredibly, in the first half of 2025 I saw 38 films in a cinema, which was exactly the same number of times I went in the second half as well. while traveling or in between trips, but in the rest of July not really. Overall I could watch some films but mostly in short bursts.

As always I started August annoyed with the recent slow months, movie-watching-wise, and with a weekly average getting dangerously close to 3. And as has been common, the tide changed, and I got into a good pace. In Week 35 specially I had the highest number of films watched in 2025, with 8, including 4 in a cinema, capped by a marvelous rewatch of Mononoke Hime in IMAX. That was also the second 4-cinema week of August, but alas, the last.

September marked the beginning of my downfall. It was the slowest month of the year, with only 6 films, none in a cinema. Friends visiting, a research collaborator visiting, then three straight weeks traveling for work. 6 months in and my work schedule is still in disarray as a consequence of this. Not helped at all by my unusually busier January and February in 2026. Only in the last couple weeks the backlog is subsiding. Which helps explain my inability to finish this yearly review when I wanted to. I recovered progressively through October and accelerated in November through December, with a weekly average in these last three months of 4.6 and over 5 for the last two months. This push was fueled specially by going a lot to movie theaters, a whooping 26 times in these three months, In only one week in these three months I did not go to a cinema, which is a sentence I wish I could write for any three-month period in my life. That one week was one with a long weekend in which I ran away from civilization. 18 of them to watch and rewatch films from 2025, with many of the hyped 2025 films opening in this period. From this period came some of my 2025 favorites: Die My Love, my current favorite, and O Agente Secreto and One Battle After Another, coming close by.

During those last months of 2025, going to the movies was a constant in my life, something to count on, which was very nice. That's what movies are for. So it was that despite the ups and downs, it's been, as always, wonderful. Here are all the films I saw in 2025.

Highlights

Note: The cards below have the date relative to that watch of the respective film, where I was then, and the rating I gave to that watch[5]. The circled arrows say that watch was a rewatch. In grey there are tags for things like if I saw the movie in a cinema, if I cried, if I dozed-off, etc.

[Jan-Feb] The Sean Baker experience



I love Sean Baker so much. It's not only that I like his films, but I also love his passion in defending cinema, how he approaches his films, how he continues to work as he thinks he should. I was very hyped for Anora, and to get in the mood I also took opportunity of a Criterion Channel series to catch up with some of his older films that I had not seen. So in a little more than two weeks I had these four films from him, including Anora twice, which I loved, but not as much as I thought I would. Still, I was just so happy with how much Baker achieved there, with how compelling this film is. That a month later Sean Baker would be so thoroughly crowned by the Hollywood establishment was unthinkable to me, but I welcomed it and was incredibly happy for him.

[Jan-Feb] Summer movies!



Cine Humberto Mauro had a great series going in January and February on summer movies. I could not go as much as I wanted but I still managed to rewatch a couple favorites and to see some films I had been meaning to for a while. Besides the delightful Summer with Monika, there was all that cinema of A Brigther Summer Day, which covers so much, at moments so overwhelmingly, that it feels thorough. But the greatest impact was from Y Tu Mamá También, a film that absolutely surprised me with its scope, its heart, its life. The film would go on to stay with me always.

[Feb] My favorite film



I said everything already in my impressions on how this movie, even with all the expectations and an underwhelming first watch from decades ago, still thoroughly floored me. Actually, this film is probably another element to explain the outlier Week 8 in the midst of an otherwise high-pace 8 week stretch. That week came immediately after seeing In the Mood for Love, and such a powerful experience calls for reflection. I did not want to watch other films before collecting my thoughts well on why this was such a religous experence. A perfect symphony. If pressed, this is the film that I would say is my favorite ever.

[Feb] A scream



A film I had missed from 2023 but that I sought out after seeing Mike D'Angelo's raves. I was not expecting what this film threw at me. It is, really, really, really, insane. Specially that scene, where a look, and a scream, which is not really a scream but a music explosion that I nevertheless will always remember as a scream, propel such force that I distinguishedly felt like time stopped. I spent the rest of 2025 suggesting this film to people whenever somebody asked me for a recommendation.

[Apr] Spaghetti westerns!



In 2024 there had been a one-off showing of Once Upon a Time in the West and it was glorious, so I was really glad when they announced a whole Spaghetti Western series at Cine Humberto Mauro, centered specially on Leone's films.

I was happy to rewatch my favorite Dollar films, the last two, for the first time in a cinema. Incredible films, but its their endings that make them so marvelous, and the boldness of their epic scopes is experienced all the better in the big screen. I actually dislike writing this sentence because I firmly believe that any film benefits from being seen in a big screen. It's just that it is even more true for Leone's films. Nothing compares to having an immense screen with a close-up of an intense glare by a magnetic actor with a thrilling score going.

I had never seen Giù La Testa, so it was interesting, but it didn't quite work for me. The series closed with the only reasonable choice. What power. I was completely transported.

[Apr] Honoring David Lynch



When Lynch left us I wanted to honor him in the only way that makes sense to me, by celebrating his movies, so I (finally) watched my Criterion 4K UHD edition of Mulholland Drive, my favorite of his movies. It was an intense, difficult experience, given what this film is. The art transcends, and Lynch does as well.

In April the film was re-released in cinemas and I of course went to see it again. It hit me harder, which I did not expect. Thank you, Lynch.

[May] Completing Almodovarization



For years I was skeptical of Almodóvar, not having liked La piel que habito when I saw it, probably around its time of release, and being unimpressed by Madres paralelas and by Extraña forma de vida. But then I watched Hable con ella in 2024, was completely taken by it, and finally felt Almodovarizado, i.e., I finally understood what people talked so much about.

None of that prepared me to how much Todo sobre mi madre would impact me. I surrended wholly to its melodrama. So bold, so vast, so outrageously real. To this day I can vividly remember Huma looking back, just before the cut to black, the electrifying sensation. Indelible.

[Aug] Mononoke Hime breaks through, capping a great week



I've always resisted this film. This was maybe my fourth or fifth time watching it. But there was always some nagging sensation after I finished it, on account of its ending, or something in between (extended beginning never let me down). So I was just so happy to finally surrender to Mononke Hime, to feel just elated by it. And seeing it in IMAX, gloriously, with Joe Hisashi's score surging through that overpowering sound. It gave me life. This also topped an amazing week, the most prolific of 2025, where I went to the cinema a bunch, saw old and new films, and just thoroughly enjoyed it.

[Oct] Uma época cheia de pirraça



A huge catalyst for my newfound movie enthusiasm in October was going to movie theaters a lot, and instrumental to that was Cine Humberto Mauro's series 1945: 80 anos depois. Rewatching Strangelove, and for the first time in a cinema, was as delightful as I expected, but doing the same for Come and See was something else. It hollowed me out, as I also expected.

Three new films from 2025 that highlighted October, in one way or another, were also touching on war, or military, themes. Lots of pirraça, as Kleber Mendonça Filho would put it. Alas, a theme ever more timely.

[Nov-Dec] Die, my love



Up until this point my favorite film from 2025 was 28 Years Later, which I loved but not as much as I thought was warranted from the top film of the year. I watched Die My Love and it did not top it, but it left me intrigued, eager for more. I had to insist. And I was greatly rewarded. It felt like an apotheosis. 2025, what a year.

Top 100 of the last 25 years (2000-2024)

Inspired my the New York Times top 100 films of the 21st century (nevermind including 2000 as if it were part of this century) I felt like doing a top 100 myself as well for the first 25 years in the 2000s.

This was a rather painful process, as it turns out, which I did mostly in early July of 2025. Another reason it took me so long to do this yearly review... Selecting some 120 films I could do quickly (from films I have rated at least an 8 since 2015 and also unrated films which I held in high steem). Getting down to 100 was tough though. One thing that made it more complicated is not only the unrated films that I may want to keep that were harder to compare with the rated ones but also the fact that I am suspicious of my own ranking. Ratings from 2015 and 2016 are hard to trust, as I was only just beginning to watch more films and could be easily impressed, and from 2017 to 2019 while I feel like the ratings got stable, inconsistencies here and there were probably still happening.

I thought about rewatching a bunch of unrated films that either I considered could shot up to the top 10 or top 20 (which I was considering of being more particular of the films to get into it, leaving all others unordered) or that I might want to include in the top 100. For the reasons discussed at length above, I never got to do that really and only a couple things changed from the initial list. I thought that maybe Black Swan, Dogville, Tree of Life, Amour, Her or Brokeback Mountain were films that I might regard more strongly now. Of those only Brokeback Mountain I had seen since 2015 but all others were films that absolutely shattered me the first time around. Some of the films that had not made it to the top 100 that I thought maybe rewatching could change things were Melancholia, Boyhood, and Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind. In the end I ended up only getting to see Black Swan, which actually made me remove it from the top 100.

So here is the top 100 and, to avoid me dragging this on forever, a top 25 in a loose order only of films I rated and that I'm only truly confident about the top 2. In the top 100 after the first 25 all others are in order of year of release / alphabetical.
